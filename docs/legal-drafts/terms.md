## Reporting a problem and taking listings down

**How to report.**

- In the app, use **Report** on a listing or on a person and choose the reason.
- Or email support@studiskarte.com. Give the link to the listing or the person's handle, say what is wrong, and give your name and a way to reach you. If you are the owner of a right (for example a trademark or copyright) that a listing breaks, say so and say how you hold that right.

**What happens next.**

- A listing reported by three different members is hidden at once and goes to the owner for review. A report by fewer members is also reviewed, but the listing stays up until the owner decides.
- Listings that look prohibited by law, unsafe or dangerous are handled first. We aim to act within 24 hours of a clear report. We aim to look at every other report within 3 working days.
- The owner may restore a listing, remove it, or suspend an account. The owner has no team, so some reviews take longer; we will say so if we need more time.
- When we hide or remove a listing, the member who posted it is told in the app. We end any reservation on a removed listing.
- If a government agency orders us to take a listing down, we will do so, and we may tell the member who posted it.
- If you reported by email, we will reply to that email with what we decided.
- We do not tell the person you reported who reported them.

**Complaints and disagreement.** If you think we got a decision wrong (a listing was removed, an account suspended, or a report ignored), email support@studiskarte.com with the details. We will answer within 7 days. ## Notes for the lawyer (delete before publishing)

- Internet Transactions Act (RA 11967), read in full on lawphil.net: Sec 3 says business-to-consumer and business-to-business transactions are covered and consumer-to-consumer (C2C) transactions are not; Sec 4(c) defines C2C as transactions between end-users for personal, family or household purposes and not in the ordinary course of business. Whether this app, and its occasional student sellers, are inside or outside the Act is the first question. These terms and the takedown procedure are written to work either way.
- If the Act applies, the obligations of platforms that do not retain oversight of the sale are in Sec 22: tell commercial from non-commercial accounts (a), prohibit regulated goods without permit information (b), require name and brand, price, description, condition and contact information in offers (c), an effective and responsive redress mechanism to report a user or information (d), a list of accounts used for e-commerce as far as practicable (e), data privacy (f). Sec 24: the internal redress mechanism must be used first, and is deemed exhausted if a complaint is unresolved after 7 calendar days. Sec 26(b): subsidiary liability if, after notice, the platform fails to act expeditiously to remove goods that infringe intellectual property or are subject of a takedown order. Sec 27: solidary liability if, after notice, it fails to act expeditiously to remove goods that are prohibited by law, imminently injurious, unsafe or dangerous. Sec 15: DTI ex parte takedown order, 48 hours to be heard, 30 days maximum.
