## 3. Language and tone

What the evidence supports:

- **Regulation requires plain language in the audience's language.** NPC Circular 2023-04 §3(C) requires "clear, plain, consistent, and straight-forward language". §3(E) requires information "in a language or dialect that an average member of the target audience can understand". §21(C) judges disclosures by what the average member of the audience understands, "taking into consideration the language that was used." <https://privacy.gov.ph/wp-content/uploads/2023/11/NPC-Circular-No.-2023-04_Guidelines-on-Consent_07Nov2023.pdf> (**strong**)
- **Taglish is the natural register of urban Filipinos.** A localisation vendor says mixing lets speakers "calibrate tone, distance, authority, or intimacy", and warns that Taglish does not translate cleanly. <https://veqta.com/the-code-switching-paradox-why-taglish-isnt-broken-english-but-a-localization-minefield> (**weak**, vendor blog; I read only the snippet)
- **The agency table recommending English for navigation and buttons and Taglish for notifications and marketing** is on <https://seraphim.vn/pages/ui-ux-design-philippines>. It states that Taglish notifications get "35% higher engagement" with no source. **Do not rely on that figure.** (**weak**)
- Regional languages exist (Cebuano and others). The same agency page says English is the safer default outside Manila. (**weak**)

I found no study that tests English versus Taglish UI copy with Filipino students. Decide by testing with real students, not from this brief.

## 7. Data privacy

### Statute

- **The Data Privacy Act of 2012 (RA 10173)** lists as sensitive personal information anything "about an individual's… age" and "health, education…", plus information "issued by government agencies peculiar to an individual". Processing sensitive information is prohibited unless an exception applies, the first being "consent… specific to the purpose prior to the processing" (§13(a)). General principles are transparency, legitimate purpose and proportionality. Data must be "adequate and not excessive" and "retained only for as long as necessary" (§11). <https://privacy.gov.ph/data-privacy-act> (**strong**)
- **Reading for Studiskarte [INFERENCE]**: a student's school and enrolment status reveal "education" and so look sensitive. A school ID image shows name, photo, school and often a number and birth data. State-university IDs could also fall under "issued by government agencies". I am not a lawyer; get this confirmed.
- **Individuals acting for personal or household affairs are excluded** from the definition of "controller" (§3(h)(2)). That exclusion covers students trading with each other, not the platform running the service.

### NPC Circular 2023-04 (Guidelines on Consent, 7 Nov 2023)

<https://privacy.gov.ph/wp-content/uploads/2023/11/NPC-Circular-No.-2023-04_Guidelines-on-Consent_07Nov2023.pdf> (**strong**). Points that bear on design:

- Consent must be freely given, specific, informed, an indication of will, and evidenced (§§7–11). It is granular. Unrelated purposes must be listed separately "instead of requiring an all-inclusive consent" (§8). Consent for processing not needed to deliver the service must not be bundled with it (§8(B)).
- Consent can never be assumed: "Non-response or implied consent does not constitute valid consent" (§10(A)).
- Just-in-time and layered notices are "the default format" (§9(C)). Consent fatigue must be minimised (§9(B)).
- No deceptive methods (§7(A)). The related NPC Advisory 2023-01 covers deceptive design patterns. <https://privacy.gov.ph/wp-content/uploads/2023/11/NPC-Advisory-No.-2023-01-Guidelines-on-Deceptive-Design-Patterns_7Nov23.pdf> (**strong**; I read only its title and purpose line).
- Withdrawal must be "as easy as, if not easier than, giving consent", through the same interface, and on withdrawal the controller must stop processing and delete (§13).
- Proportionality: collect only what is "directly relevant and necessary" (§5(D)). Keep only enough data to prove consent was obtained (§12(A)).
- Validity lapses if the processing changes considerably; new consent is needed (§21). Consent is "an actively managed choice", not a one-time compliance step (§21(D)).
- Public availability of data is not blanket consent for any use (§17).
- Automated processing or profiling must be disclosed, with safeguards against discriminatory outcomes (§18).

I found no age threshold for valid consent in this Circular. For under-18s, NPC's Sept 2026 advisory reminds platforms of "heightened responsibilities" for children's data and calls for "privacy-preserving age-assurance" and an end to behavioural profiling of children. <https://privacy.gov.ph/public-advisory-npc-calls-on-digital-platforms-to-ensure-compliance> (**strong** for what it says; it is about minors generally, not college marketplaces). Many first-year students are 17 [INFERENCE: K-12 graduates are typically 17–18; no source opened].

### ID photos and selfies

- **NPC warned in August 2023** against photographing IDs on personal devices or sending ID photos over Viber, WhatsApp or Messenger without safeguards. It requires explicit consent, a clear privacy notice (purposes, security, retention, purpose limit), secure storage and transmission, and disposal "once the purpose is fulfilled". <https://newsbytes.ph/2023/08/05/npc-warns-firms-villages-against-indiscriminate-taking-photos-of-ids> (**medium**, trade press of an NPC statement; the NPC page itself was not opened)
- **Grab Philippines selfie case (NPC CC 20-001).** NPC ordered Grab to stop selfie verification and audio and video recording in February 2020. <https://privacy.gov.ph/npc-suspends-grab-phs-selfie-verification-audio-video-recording-systems> (**strong**; only the title and snippet were read). In its 8 Jan 2024 Order, NPC found Grab's 40-year retention of selfies "excessive for failure to justify the same by showing legal basis", and said retention should run "from the time the users' account with Grab PH has been terminated and not from the time of collection". It also faulted Grab's assessment for being unclear on whether selfies were kept "as long as necessary" or up to a 10-year maximum. Grab had cut the maximum to ten years for users involved in safety incidents. <https://privacy.gov.ph/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/NPC-CC-20-001-01.08.2024-In-re-Grab-Philippines-Order-1.pdf> (**strong**)
- **NPC treats biometrics as permanent identifiers.** In October 2025 it issued a cease-and-desist order against Tools For Humanity (Worldcoin), quoting that "the integrity of a Filipino citizen's biometric data is non-negotiable". <https://privacy.gov.ph/npc-issues-cease-and-desist-order-against-tools-for-humanity> (**strong**; snippet only)
- **A breach probe at GCash (Oct 2025)** shows NPC investigating reported leaks even where the company denies them. <https://business.inquirer.net/554778/gcash-denies-data-breach-as-privacy-commission-starts-probe> (**medium**)

## 8. Design implications

Each item states the design consequence, the evidence, and a rating of how well the evidence supports it. Items marked [INFERENCE] go beyond the sources.

1. **Treat Facebook and Messenger as the incumbent.** Compare every feed, chat and listing flow with a Facebook group or Marketplace thread, because 81.9% of the population (95.8M) and 83.3% of adults use them. Keep thread-based chat with the product pinned on top (already built). Source: <https://datareportal.com/reports/digital-2026-philippines>. Rating: **strong** for reach; the design consequence is [INFERENCE].
2. **Design at 360 px for a 2GB-RAM Android, and test on one.** Android is about 86–87% of mobile traffic and low-cost brands lead. Keep pages light, compress photos, avoid autoplay, reserve image space. Sources: <https://gs.statcounter.com/os-market-share/mobile/philippines>, <https://www.statista.com/statistics/938806/philippines-market-share-of-leading-mobile-brands/>. Rating: **medium**. The specific device class (2GB RAM) is **weak** (<https://seraphim.vn/pages/ui-ux-design-philippines>).
3. **Count data cost into photo handling.** Show a size cue before upload and resize in the browser, since data is prepaid. Sources: <https://seraphim.vn/pages/ui-ux-design-philippines>, <https://www.yugatech.com/news/globe-outs-go-prepaid-promo-starting-at-5gb-for-php-50>. Rating: **weak** (agency page, 2019 promo). The faster median mobile speed (59.64 Mbps) argues the other way, so measure before deciding.
4. **Do not make push the only alert path.** iPhone push works only after Home Screen install and a user-tap subscription, and some Android OEMs show push quietly. Keep the in-app notification list authoritative and teach the iPhone install step (already on the Me page). Sources: <https://webkit.org/blog/13878/web-push-for-web-apps-on-ios-and-ipados/>, <https://issues.chromium.org/issues/506912902>. Rating: **strong** for iOS; **weak** for the Samsung and Xiaomi behaviour.
5. **Time notifications and reminders to evenings, and listing prompts to the afternoon.** Carousell sees 9–11 p.m. as peak buying and chatting, and 2 p.m. as peak listing. Use this as a default for digest timing, then replace it with Studiskarte's own data. Source: <https://press.carousell.com/2024/11/26/ukay-ukay-goes-digital-on-carousell-filipinos-lead-the-region-in-secondhand-fashion-transactions-gen-z-users-brings-back-retro-2000s-digital-cameras>. Rating: **medium** (first-party, but Carousell's own audience, not students).
6. **Drive seasonal features from each school's calendar, not a national one.** TUP starts 29 Jul, Ateneo 5 Aug, some schools June, with midyears and intersessions. Store start and end dates per school domain and use them for "start-of-term" and "end-of-term clear-out" shelves (textbooks, uniforms, dorm items). Sources: <https://tup.edu.ph/viewcalendar>, <https://www.ateneo.edu/registrar/academic-calendar/2026-2027>. Rating: **strong** for the calendar facts; the resale seasonality is [INFERENCE] with only weak anecdote (<https://www.facebook.com/psitsusmmain/>).
7. **Offer textbooks and uniforms as first-class categories, and "free / give away" as a visible option.** Campus Facebook groups already host this. Carousell reports clothes as the most-given-away item. Sources: <https://www.facebook.com/groups/2303430809699968>, <https://press.carousell.com/2024/11/26/ukay-ukay-goes-digital-on-carousell-filipinos-lead-the-region-in-secondhand-fashion-transactions-gen-z-users-brings-back-retro-2000s-digital-cameras>. Rating: **weak** for campus (group posts), **medium** for the Carousell fact.
8. **Make trust cues about authenticity and condition prominent.** 72% of never-second-hand buyers said authentication and warranty would motivate them. Keep condition, photos of flaws, and seller reply time and stars visible on the card. Source: <https://press.carousell.com/2021/12/01/carousell-report-finds-philippines-most-receptiveto-secondhand-in-the-region>. Rating: **medium**.
9. **Keep GCash as a declared payment option, with a "confirm in your app" prompt on the payee side.** The payee must check the wallet's transaction history, never a screenshot. Show this exactly when someone taps "Payment received", and keep the payee's account note visible only to the two people in the deal (already built). Source: <https://mynt.com.ph/newsroom/gcash-warns-public-on-emerging-ai-generated-fake-receipts-scams-urges-users-to-always-check-transactions-tab>. Rating: **strong**.
10. **Keep cash-at-meet-up as a first-class option.** Cash on delivery is still about 23% of e-commerce value, the highest in six markets, and 12% of Filipino respondents say they will never switch. Source: <https://newsbytes.ph/2026/10/17/cod-remains-dominant-in-ph-e-commerce-despite-digital-payment-growth/>. Rating: **medium**.
11. **Put the non-delivery scam at the centre of safety copy.** About 86% of 2024 consumer-fraud complaints were goods or services paid for and never delivered, so teach "do not pay before the handover is agreed", and keep reserve-then-meet. The 2024 share is the latest published. Source: <https://sulat.ph/data/online-scams> citing CICC. Rating: **medium**.
12. **Plan for in-app recourse because outside redress may not exist.** If DTI cannot act on non-registered sellers, reports, hiding by three reporters, and the admin queue are the main remedy for students. Say so plainly in the guidelines, and tell students how to preserve evidence and where to report (PNP Anti-Cybercrime Group, NBI Cybercrime Division, GCash "Report a Scam"). Sources: <https://www.pna.gov.ph/articles/1268940> (snippet only), <https://mynt.com.ph/newsroom/gcash-warns-public-on-emerging-ai-generated-fake-receipts-scams-urges-users-to-always-check-transactions-tab>, <https://www.lawyer-philippines.com/articles/what-to-do-if-scammed-on-online-marketplaces-like-facebook-marketplace-in-the-philippines>. Rating: **weak** for the DTI claim, **medium** for the reporting routes.
13. **Suggest meet-up places without inventing norms.** Prefer a place field with suggested public, daytime, on-campus or mall options, lit and busy, and let each school add its own spots. I found no Philippine source that says which places Filipino students use or that police-supervised exchange spots exist here. Source: absence of evidence, see section 6. Rating: **weak** [INFERENCE].
14. **Use English for navigation, buttons and errors, and let warm, low-stakes copy lean Taglish, but test it.** Plain language is a legal requirement for privacy notices (NPC §3(C), §3(E)). No study tests English against Taglish UI copy. Sources: <https://privacy.gov.ph/wp-content/uploads/2023/11/NPC-Circular-No.-2023-04_Guidelines-on-Consent_07Nov2023.pdf>, <https://veqta.com/the-code-switching-paradox-why-taglish-isnt-broken-english-but-a-localization-minefield>, <https://seraphim.vn/pages/ui-ux-design-philippines>. Rating: **strong** for the plain-language rule; **weak** for the Taglish register advice. Ignore the unsourced "35% higher engagement" claim.
15. **Keep email-code verification until a stronger step is justified.** Collecting ID images is sensitive processing that needs specific prior consent and a defensible retention period. The current `.edu.ph` code sends nothing sensitive beyond the email and school domain. Sources: <https://privacy.gov.ph/data-privacy-act> (§§11, 13), <https://newsbytes.ph/2023/08/05/npc-warns-firms-villages-against-indiscriminate-taking-photos-of-ids>. Rating: **strong** for the law; that email is "enough" is [INFERENCE].
16. **If ID or selfie verification is added later, design it around deletion.** Show a just-in-time notice at the capture screen (purpose, who sees it, how long, how to withdraw). Keep a pass or fail flag, date and method, not the image, and delete the image once the check is done. Never ask for ID or selfie over chat. Per NPC's Grab order, measure any retention period from account termination, not from collection, and justify its length. Sources: <https://privacy.gov.ph/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/NPC-CC-20-001-01.08.2024-In-re-Grab-Philippines-Order-1.pdf>, <https://privacy.gov.ph/wp-content/uploads/2023/11/NPC-Circular-No.-2023-04_Guidelines-on-Consent_07Nov2023.pdf> (§§5, 8, 9(C), 12(A), 13). Rating: **strong**. The "delete the image after the check" design is a proportionality reading [INFERENCE].
17. **Make consent granular, optional where possible, and as easy to withdraw as to give.** Separate switches for push alerts, perks sharing with partners, and analytics. No pre-ticked boxes, and no consent bundled into terms acceptance. Put withdrawal in Settings next to where it was given. Source: NPC Circular 2023-04 §§8, 10, 13 at the URL above. Rating: **strong**.
18. **Share with perk partners only what each partner needs, with a separate yes.** A partner that asks for a student's school or status is receiving education data. Give a layered notice naming the partner at the point of sharing. Sources: NPC Circular 2023-04 §15 (data sharing) at the URL above; DPA §13 at <https://privacy.gov.ph/data-privacy-act>. Rating: **strong** for the rules; the application to Perks is [INFERENCE].
19. **Decide the under-18 position now, before roles and ID verification.** NPC's September 2026 advisory asks platforms for privacy-preserving age assurance and no profiling of children. The DPA lists age as sensitive. Source: <https://privacy.gov.ph/public-advisory-npc-calls-on-digital-platforms-to-ensure-compliance>. Rating: **strong** that NPC is focused here; whether it applies to a `.edu.ph` marketplace is [INFERENCE].
20. **Do not rely on a school ID alone as proof of student status when you add roles.** Philippine law itself backs a school ID with a government ID or validated enrolment form for the fare discount, and penalises falsified IDs. Faculty, staff, alumni and visitors will need separate trust tiers and labels. Source: <https://lawphil.net/statutes/repacts/ra2019/ra_11314_2019.html> (§§5, 11). Rating: **strong** for the statute; the design consequence is [INFERENCE]. The scale of identity theft (1,626 CICC complaints in 2025, <https://sulat.ph/data/online-scams>, **medium**) supports caution.
21. **Expect a first-time-seller audience and keep selling cheap in effort.** Almost every Filipino respondent in Carousell's survey had bought second-hand, and sellers there earn a meaningful sum (average ₱39,000). Keep posting under a minute, with defaults for category and price. Sources: <https://press.carousell.com/2021/12/01/carousell-report-finds-philippines-most-receptiveto-secondhand-in-the-region>, <https://press.carousell.com/2024/11/26/ukay-ukay-goes-digital-on-carousell-filipinos-lead-the-region-in-secondhand-fashion-transactions-gen-z-users-brings-back-retro-2000s-digital-cameras>. Rating: **medium** (Carousell users only).
22. **Watch for a gender gap in selling.** A national survey found men out-earned women in online sales. If Studiskarte tracks sellers, report items sold and earnings by gender only if the owner wants it, and make sure reviews and "typically replies within" do not penalise women or part-time sellers unfairly. Source: <https://www.adb.org/publications/harnessing-the-potential-of-online-marketplaces-in-the-philippines-insights-from-the-national-information-and-communications-technology-household-survey>. Rating: **strong** for the finding (abstract only); the design consequence is [INFERENCE].

## 9. Questions only the owner can answer

1. **What is the Studiskarte card?** Who issues it, is it physical or digital, who are the partners, and does the marketplace's Perks tab verify holders by card, by school email, or both? I found nothing public to build on.
2. **Which schools are in scope?** Only state universities with `.edu.ph` addresses, or private schools and trimester schools too? This sets the calendar model and whether ID images could count as "government-issued".
3. **Are under-18 students allowed?** If yes, what is the plan for consent and age assurance?
4. **Will you add ID or selfie verification, and for whom** (visitors, alumni, faculty)? If yes, who is the data protection officer, and have you registered with the National Privacy Commission?
5. **What retention period will you commit to for any ID image or selfie,** and from what date does it run (account closure, per the Grab order, is the safest reading)?
6. **English only, or Taglish in some copy, or a Filipino option?** Should I test with students first?
7. **How should the app talk about GCash and cash?** Do you want the app to name GCash and show the "check your transactions tab" warning, or stay neutral about payment brands?
8. **Which meet-up places will you recommend,** and are any schools willing to name a guarded or CCTV-covered exchange spot?
9. **Do you have access to campus Facebook group admins or student orgs** who could tell us what sells at the start and end of each term?
